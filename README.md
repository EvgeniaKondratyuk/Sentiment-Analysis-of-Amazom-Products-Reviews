## Sentiment-Analysis-of-Amazom-Products-Reviews
An analysis of the impact of positive and negative reviews on Amazon products.
Do negative reviews have more weight at the time of making a decision? What influences consumers' behaviour more?

Nowadays, potential consumers have at their disposal numerous websites with a wide range of
options to purchase products and services. Sometimes, making the choice is overwhelming and it
pushes them to rely more and more on reviews left by other consumers. Recent studies show that
online reviews not only influence buyers' decision-making but also that customers trust this type of
feedback more than other traditional sources of information (Bickart and Schindler, 2001). In this
study, we examine this aforementioned impact, specifically that negative reviews have a stronger
influence on potential consumers’ decisions than positive as other researchers have suggested (Chiu
et al., 2019).

To analyse consumer preferences, a corpus of online reviews of Amazon customers is created. To
identify linguistic features, such as individual words and phrases used by customers to evaluate a
product or service, the corpus is subjected to data pre-processing, including tokenisation, removal of
stop words, and sentimental analysis. The result of the analysis reveals linguistic entities classified
into three categories: positive reviews, neutral reviews and negative reviews.

The research methodology is based on an online survey of the general public of users of Internet
resources of products and services. The survey includes reviews with key linguistic features of
positive, neutral and negative nature of ten questions each. Respondents are asked to assess on a
subjective basis if a particular phrase in the review has an impact on their decision to purchase a
product or service. Respondents select a response on a rating scale from 0 to 10 points, where 10 is
“would purchase” and 0 is “would not purchase”.

The research has an informative nature. The results of this study could be usefull for companies of
various business sectors that use an online review system for customers' feedback on products by
further implementation in marketing analysis and sales strategies. Moreover, Whether our
hypothesis is true or not, this study contributes to a broader understanding of consumer behavior.
